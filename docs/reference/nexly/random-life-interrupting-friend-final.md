This is a delicate conversation, but it's worth having — close friendships can handle honesty if it's framed as care rather than criticism. The goal isn't to win a point; it's to make the dynamic better for both of you.

**Pick the right moment.** Not in the heat of an interruption, and not in front of others. A calm, private setting (a walk, coffee, a quiet text if you're apart) lowers defenses.

**Use "I notice" + "I feel" + "I'd love."**  
> "Hey, I've noticed I often lose my train of thought when we're talking because I get cut off. It makes me feel like what I'm saying doesn't matter. I'd love it if we could let each other finish — I think we'd both get more out of our conversations."

**Name the pattern, not the person.**  
"You interrupt me a lot" sounds like an accusation. "I've noticed our conversations often overlap" names the dynamic without blame.

**Give a concrete, low-stakes signal.**  
Agree on a gentle cue — a raised finger, a light hand on the arm, saying "hold that thought" — so either of you can pause the other without it feeling like a reprimand.

**Acknowledge the why.**  
Often interruptions come from excitement, not disrespect. "I know you jump in because you're engaged, and I love that energy — I just need a beat to land my thought first."

**Check in after.**  
A week later: "Hey, how's that going for you? I've been trying to leave more space too." It keeps it collaborative.

If they get defensive, you can say: "I'm bringing this up because I value us, not because I'm annoyed. Can we try it for a bit and see how it feels?"
